Ballanheim is a queer upcycling label from Berlin, run by Andrew, who turns discarded clothes into one-off pieces. I photographed the first campaign, built the identity around a single struck seal and wrote the website, so everything the label puts out is its own.

I met Andrew when he was working a shift at the local Humana in one of his own outfits. The shop is also where a lot of his inspiration comes from, the locals who build a whole look out of worn second-hand pieces.

It took me about a minute to see that the style was not the only thing that was his, the vision was too. Everything he makes is built from recycled material.

When I interviewed him for the website, I learned that his goal is to make something deeply connected to his Scottish heritage and at the same time something rioting against the old scheme of things, which might be quite a common statement in Berlin nowadays.

Clothes, in his opinion, should have no gender, should be as comfortable as it gets and should work on the street as well as on the dancefloor, carrying fresh energy and rediscovered traditional patterns from his home country.

For the campaign he only wanted daylight, and a flash once it got too dark, to keep it raw.

My goal was to capture that eternal summer we met in, when everything seemed possible for a young aspiring artist.

Zola, whose creations you can see in the photos, was looking for a photographer who could catch a glimpse of queer, free joy in her parents' fabric factory.

@bbyaga_zola

Surrounded by fashion from a young age, she has always known she wanted to create something unapologetically different.

Her clothes, as weird and unusual as they are, represent the energy she has found in Berlin over the past couple of years.

There are not that many pictures here, but they hold what we were after. A wild girl who needs no one and is on the hunt.

On a frosty day in the middle of the Berlin winter we spent hours together, working out how to portray the strong woman she is.

I wanted to catch her lying in wait for prey, with a face closer to a wild animal than to a subtle, fragile girly girl.

Ulia remains one of my role models. She is one of the strongest and most creative people I know, and never short of a new idea.

My name is Krzysztof Wiatr and I am originally from Szczecin, a smaller city near the German and Polish border.

I broke off my medical studies and boldly decided to move to Berlin to pursue my artistic goals, where I finished an Ausbildung as a Mediengestalter.

I have been taking pictures for the past couple of years and I am currently open for new career opportunities.
